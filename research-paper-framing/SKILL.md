# Research Paper Framing

Use this skill to clarify a manuscript's contribution before polishing its language. If the novelty claim is vague, overstated, unsupported, or weaker than the draft implies, say so directly and tighten the claim before writing.

Identify the venue or genre, then extract the actual research object: problem, setting, assumptions, method primitive, theorem or empirical result, evidence, application, limitation, and closest baseline. Build a literature map before making novelty claims. Separate mature background, neighboring work, direct competitors, and the exact gap or obstruction the manuscript addresses.

Frame the contribution in contrastive form: what existing approaches do, why that is insufficient here, what the manuscript changes, what result follows, and what evidence supports it. An abstract should read as a compressed argument: problem, contrast, core result, consequence, and application. An introduction should narrow from field context to bottleneck, prior approaches, unresolved gap, proposed direction, contributions, and paper organization.

Read `references/paper-framing-patterns.md` for paragraph templates, contribution patterns, citation discipline, and venue-facing checklists.

## Citation Discipline

Never invent references, DOIs, venues, page numbers, author lists, or publication years. When the user asks for literature research, DOI filtering, clickable references, or bibliography updates, verify metadata against authoritative sources before editing. Every citation must have a role; do not inflate the bibliography with loosely related work.

## Style And Verification

Use professional academic prose with concrete nouns and explicit logical links. Avoid hype, decorative complexity, metaphors, generic field summaries, and unsupported priority claims. When the user provides a model paper, imitate only its argumentative function and section rhythm, not its wording or sentence structure.

For LaTeX projects, check undefined citations, missing bibliography entries, repeated or unused references when relevant, abstract length, title consistency, hyperlink support, overfull boxes near edited text, and user-specified style constraints.
